1Rectilinear motion: variable and constant acceleration

For variable acceleration use the differential forms and integrate between limits.

a = dv/dt  ⇒  ∫v1v2 dv = ∫t1t2 a dt
a = v dv/dx  ⇒  ∫v1v2 v dv = ∫x1x2 a dx

For constant a, integrate once and twice:

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
s = ½(u + v)t
Eg. u = 10 m/s, a = 2 m/s², t = 5 s → v = 20 m/s, s = 75 m.

2Relative motion of particles

rA/B = rA − rB
vA/B = vA − vB
aA/B = aA − aB

Use a vector triangle or components. Example: a boat heads across a river at 5 m/s while the current is 3 m/s downstream.

Eg. Resultant v = √(5²+3²) = 5.83 m/s at 31° to the bank; to cross 100 m takes 20 s and the boat drifts 60 m downstream.

3D'Alembert's principle

Write the inertia force Fi = −ma and add it to the free-body diagram; the system is then in static equilibrium.

ΣF + (−ma) = 0  or  ΣF = ma
LiftTension
upward acceleration aT = m(g + a)
downward acceleration aT = m(g − a)

Eg. m = 100 kg, a = 2 m/s² up → T = 1181 N; down → T = 781 N.

4Connected bodies and pulleys

For two masses m1 > m2 connected by a light inextensible string over a frictionless pulley:

m1g − T = m1a
T − m2g = m2a
a = (m1 − m2)g/(m1 + m2)
T = 2m1m2g/(m1 + m2)
Eg. m1 = 5 kg, m2 = 3 kg → a = 2.45 m/s², T = 36.8 N.

For a block on a rough incline, use D'Alembert along and perpendicular to the plane; the string tension is the same on both sides of a light frictionless pulley.

6Projectile motion

Horizontal velocity is constant; vertical motion has a = −g.

x = u cos θ · t,   y = u sin θ · t − ½gt²
y = x tan θ − gx²/(2u²cos²θ)
T = 2u sin θ/g,   H = u²sin²θ/(2g),   R = u²sin 2θ/g
Eg. u = 20 m/s, θ = 30° → R = 35.3 m, H = 5.1 m, T = 2.04 s.

7Impulse and momentum

p = mv,   J = ∫F dt = Δp = mv2 − mv1

The impulse is the area under the force-time graph. For a constant force, J = F Δt; average force Favg = Δp/Δt.

Eg. 0.5 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s and rebounds at 6 m/s: take right positive, Δp = 0.5(−6−10) = −8 N·s (8 N·s to the left). Over 0.02 s, Favg = 400 N.

Momentum is conserved in a system when the net external impulse is zero.

8Impact and coefficient of restitution

For direct central impact, momentum is conserved along the line of impact. The coefficient of restitution compares separation and approach speeds:

m1v1 + m2v2 = m1v1' + m2v2'
e = (v2' − v1')/(v1 − v2)

e = 0 perfectly plastic (stick together); e = 1 perfectly elastic; 0 < e < 1 partial.

Eg. m1 = 2 kg, v1 = 5 m/s; m2 = 3 kg, v2 = 0; e = 0.5 → v1' = 0.5 m/s, v2' = 3 m/s.

9D'Alembert on an incline

Block of mass m on an incline θ, force P up the plane, friction μ. Perpendicular to the plane, N = mg cos θ; along the plane, P − mg sin θ − f = ma, with f = μN.

a = [P − mg sin θ − μmg cos θ]/m
Eg. m = 10 kg, θ = 30°, μ = 0.2, P = 100 N: N = 84.96 N, f = 16.99 N, a = 3.40 m/s².

If the block moves down, reverse the friction direction; if a comes out negative, the assumed motion is wrong.

10Atwood machine by work-energy

For masses m1 > m2 moving through height h:

(m1 − m2)gh = ½(m1 + m2)v²
v = √(2gh(m1−m2)/(m1+m2))
Eg. 5 kg and 3 kg, h = 2 m → v = 3.13 m/s; the tension is T = 2m1m2g/(m1+m2) = 36.8 N.

The work done by gravity on the heavier mass minus the work against gravity on the lighter mass equals the gain in kinetic energy of both.

For downward motion from rest, v = gt, s = ½gt², v² = 2gs.

12Curvilinear motion: normal and tangential acceleration

Velocity is tangent to the path. Its magnitude change gives tangential acceleration; its direction change gives normal (centripetal) acceleration.

at = dv/dt,   an = v²/ρ
a = √(at² + an²)

Circle: at = αr, an = v²/r = ω²r. Radius of curvature: ρ = [1+(dy/dx)²]3/2/|d²y/dx²|.

Eg. Car on r = 50 m at v = 15 m/s: an = 4.5 m/s²; with braking at = −2 m/s², total a = 4.92 m/s².

13Potential energy and conservation

A conservative force has a potential: F = −dV/dx. Gravity V = mgh; spring V = ½kx².

T1 + V1 = T2 + V2
T1 + V1 + Unc = T2 + V2
Eg. 2 kg released from 5 m: mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh) = 9.9 m/s. A spring k = 200 N/m compressed 0.1 m stores 1 J.

Power: P = dW/dt; for a spring/mass system, energy oscillates between kinetic and potential.

14Elastic impact and ballistic pendulum

For perfectly elastic direct impact (e = 1), both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved:

v1' = [(m1−m2)u1 + 2m2u2]/(m1+m2)
v2' = [2m1u1 + (m2−m1)u2]/(m1+m2)

Ballistic pendulum: a bullet m embeds in a block M. Momentum: mv = (m+M)V; then energy: ½(m+M)V² = (m+M)gh.

v = (m+M)/m · √(2gh)
Eg. m = 0.02 kg, M = 2 kg, h = 0.1 m → V = 1.40 m/s, v = 141 m/s.

15Work by a spring and variable force

Wspring = ½kx1² − ½kx2²
W = ∫F dx

For a force F = 3x² N from x = 0 to 2 m: W = [x³]02 = 8 J. If a 2 kg body starts from rest, v = √(2W/m) = 2.83 m/s.

Eg. Spring k = 200 N/m compressed 0.4 m stores 16 J; a 2 kg block released reaches v = 4 m/s.

Spring force: F = −kx; the negative sign shows restoration toward equilibrium.

16Recoil and force-time graphs

For a gun-bullet system, total momentum is conserved:

mbvb + MgVg = 0
J = area under the F-t graph = Δp
Eg. Bullet 0.02 kg at 400 m/s, gun 5 kg: Vg = −1.6 m/s (recoil). If the force acts for 0.02 s, the average force on the bullet is 400 N.

For a varying force, integrate the F-t curve; impulse is the area, not the peak force.

17Kinematics: position, velocity, acceleration

Rectilinear motion is along a straight line; curvilinear motion is along a curved path. Position r, displacement Δr, velocity v = dr/dt, acceleration a = dv/dt = d²r/dt².

vavg = Δr/Δt,   aavg = Δv/Δt
v = dr/dt,   a = dv/dt
Eg. x = 2t³ − 3t² + 4t: v = 6t² − 6t + 4, a = 12t − 6; at t = 2 s, v = 16 m/s, a = 18 m/s².

Graphs: slope of x-t = v; slope of v-t = a; area under v-t = displacement; area under a-t = change in velocity.

18Work-energy theorem

For a particle, F = ma = m v dv/dx. Integrate along the path:

∫F dx = ∫m v dv = ½mv2² − ½mv1²
U = ΔT = T2 − T1,   T = ½mv²
Eg. 10 kg from rest under 50 N over 4 m: U = 200 J → v = √(2U/m) = 6.32 m/s.

Work is a scalar; the theorem relates the net work to the change in kinetic energy, independent of the path shape.

19Impact energy loss and oblique impact

For direct central impact, the loss of kinetic energy is

ΔE = ½ (m1m2/(m1+m2)) (v1−v2)² (1−e²)
Eg. For the previous impact: ΔE = 11.25 J (initial 25 J, final 13.75 J).

Oblique impact: resolve velocities along and perpendicular to the line of impact. The tangential components are unchanged (smooth spheres); the normal components satisfy momentum conservation and the restitution equation.

20Dependent motion of connected particles

An inextensible rope imposes a constraint on the displacements. Write the length of the rope and differentiate.

sA + 2sB = L
vA + 2vB = 0,   aA + 2aB = 0

This is a rope over a fixed pulley and around a movable pulley carrying B; for a light frictionless movable pulley, TA = 2TB.

Eg. A moves down at 2 m/s → B moves up at 1 m/s; A accelerates at 3 m/s² → B accelerates at 1.5 m/s².

21Variable acceleration: worked example

Given v = 3t² − 2t m/s:

a = dv/dt = 6t − 2
s = ∫v dt = t³ − t² + s0
Eg. At t = 2 s (s0=0): v = 8 m/s, a = 10 m/s², s = 4 m. The area under the v-t curve from 0 to 2 s is also 4 m.

If a is given as a function of x, use a = v dv/dx and integrate with respect to x.

22Horizontal projectile

For a body projected horizontally with speed u from height h:

t = √(2h/g)
R = u t = u√(2h/g)
vy = gt,   v = √(u² + vy²)
Eg. h = 20 m, u = 10 m/s → t = 2.02 s, R = 20.2 m; impact speed v = √(100 + 392) = 22.2 m/s.

The horizontal motion is uniform; the vertical motion is free fall. The two components are independent.
