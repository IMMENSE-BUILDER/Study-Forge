2Gears, pulleys and belts

At a no-slip contact, the tangential velocity and tangential acceleration are equal:

v = ω1r1 = ω2r2
ω2 = ω1r1/r2,   α2 = α1r1/r2

For a belt drive, the same relation uses the pitch radii; the belt speed is v = ωr. A gear pair reverses the direction of rotation; an open belt keeps the same direction.

Eg. r1 = 0.2 m, r2 = 0.5 m, ω1 = 30 rad/s → ω2 = 12 rad/s; with α1 = 6 rad/s², α2 = 2.4 rad/s².

3General plane motion

Any plane motion is a translation of the centre of mass G plus a rotation about G. For two points A and B:

vB = vA + ω × rB/A
aB = aA + α × rB/A − ω²rB/A

Use relative velocity when velocities of two points are known; use the IC when the body has a simple instantaneous geometry; use relative acceleration for normal and tangential components.

Eg. A ladder sliding on a floor and wall is general plane motion: the IC is at the intersection of the perpendiculars to the end velocities.

A rigid body has 6 degrees of freedom in 3D (3 translations + 3 rotations), reduced by constraints such as a fixed axis or a roller.

Eg. A wheel rolling without slipping has one independent coordinate (θ); the centre moves x = rθ.

7Instantaneous centre of zero velocity

The instantaneous centre (IC) is the point of the body (or its extension) with zero velocity at that instant. Locate it by drawing perpendiculars to the velocities of two points; their intersection is the IC.

vA = ω · IA,   vB = ω · IB,   ω = vA/IA = vB/IB
Eg. Bar length L = 2 m, end A on the floor with vA = 3 m/s, end B on the wall, θ = 30°: ω = vA/(L sinθ) = 3 rad/s; vB = ωL cosθ = 5.2 m/s.

8Work-energy for rigid bodies

T = ½mvG² + ½IGω²
Fixed axis:   T = ½IOω²
P = Mω = Fv

Work done by a couple is U = ∫M dθ; for a constant moment, U = Mθ. The work-energy equation is U = ΔT.

Eg. Disk m = 10 kg, r = 0.5 m, ω = 4 rad/s, rolling with vG = 2 m/s: I = 1.25, T = 20 + 10 = 30 J.

9Composite bodies and parallel axis

For a composite body, add or subtract the moments of inertia of the parts about the same axis. Move each part from its own centroid to the required axis with I = IG + md².

Eg. Two rods, each m = 1 kg, L = 1 m, joined at their ends: for each rod about the joint, I = 1/3 kg·m²; total I = 2/3 kg·m².

Radius of gyration: k = √(I/m); it is the distance at which the whole mass could be concentrated to give the same I.

10Rolling with and without slipping

Pure rolling: vG = ωr, contact point at rest. If vG ≠ ωr, the contact slips and kinetic friction acts.

ΣF = maG,   ΣMG = IGα,   f = μkN

Friction opposes the relative motion at the contact. A driven wheel can have friction forward; a freely rolling wheel has friction opposing slip.

Eg. r = 0.4 m, vG = 6 m/s, ω = 10 rad/s → ωr = 4 m/s ≠ 6 m/s; the contact slips with relative speed 2 m/s.

11Relative acceleration of two points

aB = aA + α × rB/A + ω × (ω × rB/A)
2D:   aB = aA + α × r − ω²r

The two rotation terms are the tangential acceleration αr and the normal acceleration ω²r directed from B toward A.

Eg. aA = 0, r = (0.3, 0.4) m, ω = 5, α = 2 rad/s² → aB = (−0.8, 0.6) + (−7.5, −10) = (−8.3, −9.4) m/s², |aB| = 12.5 m/s².

12Rigid-body equations of motion

ΣF = m aG
ΣMG = IGα
Fixed axis through O:   ΣMO = IOα

For general plane motion, the two force equations give aGx, aGy and the moment equation gives α. For fixed-axis rotation, the axis reaction does not appear in the moment equation about O.

Eg. Disk m = 10 kg, r = 0.5 m, torque 5 N·m: I = ½mr² = 1.25 kg·m², α = 5/1.25 = 4 rad/s²; rim at = 2 m/s².

13Angular impulse and momentum

HG = IGω,   HO = IOω
∫M dt = ΔH = H2 − H1

Angular momentum is conserved about a point when the external moment about that point is zero (e.g. a freely spinning flywheel).

Eg. Flywheel I = 2 kg·m², ω1 = 10 rad/s, constant torque 5 N·m for 3 s: H2 = 20 + 15 = 35 kg·m²/s, ω2 = 17.5 rad/s.

14Conservation of angular momentum

If the external moment about an axis is zero, the angular momentum about that axis is conserved.

I1ω1 = (I1 + I2)ω2
Eg. Disk I1 = 2 kg·m² at ω1 = 15 rad/s; a second disk I2 = 3 kg·m² is dropped on it → ω2 = 6 rad/s; energy lost = 225 − 90 = 135 J.

Angular momentum is a vector along the axis; the energy loss in a perfectly inelastic rotational collision is ½(I1I2/(I1+I2))(ω1−ω2)².

16Pulley with two masses: energy

For an Atwood machine with a pulley of inertia I and radius r, use work-energy with v = ωr:

(m1−m2)gh = ½(m1+m2)v² + ½Iω²
v = √(2(m1−m2)gh/(m1+m2+I/r²))
Eg. m1 = 5 kg, m2 = 3 kg, h = 2 m, I = 0.5 kg·m², r = 0.2 m → v = 1.96 m/s.

The pulley inertia appears as an effective mass I/r² in the denominator; a massless pulley gives the simple Atwood result.

17Relative velocity of two points

For points A and B on the same rigid body:

vB = vA + ω × rB/A
2D:   ω × r = (−ω ry, ω rx)

vB/A is perpendicular to AB and has magnitude ω·AB; it represents rotation about A.

Eg. vA = (2, 0) m/s, ω = 5 rad/s, rB/A = (0.3, 0.4) m → vB = (2, 0) + (−2, 1.5) = (0, 1.5) m/s.

The velocity of A can be found from another point; choose the point whose velocity is known or whose direction is known.

18Rolling motion

For rolling without slipping on a fixed surface, the contact point is the instantaneous centre:

vG = ωr,   aG = αr
vtop = 2vG,   vcontact = 0
Eg. r = 0.4 m, vG = 6 m/s → ω = 15 rad/s; with aG = 2 m/s², α = 5 rad/s²; top speed 12 m/s.

19Translation kinetics: block on an incline

For translation, every point has the same acceleration; use the centre of mass.

N = mg cos θ,   f = μN
a = g(sin θ − μ cos θ)   (sliding down)
Eg. θ = 30°, μ = 0.2 → a = 9.81(0.5 − 0.2×0.866) = 3.21 m/s²; if tan θ < μ, the block remains at rest.

For a body pushed up the plane, friction reverses and a = −g(sin θ + μ cos θ).

20Fixed-axis rotation with a hanging mass

A pulley/disk of moment of inertia I and radius r carries a hanging mass m. Equations: mg − T = ma, Tr = Iα, a = αr.

a = mg/(m + I/r²)
T = Ia/r² = mgI/(mr² + I)
Eg. m = 2 kg, I = 0.5 kg·m², r = 0.2 m → a = 1.35 m/s², T = 16.9 N.

The pulley reduces the acceleration compared with free fall; a massless pulley (I = 0) gives a = g.

21Angular impulse on a rotating bar

∫M dt = ΔH = I(ω2 − ω1),   M = Fd

A force applied at distance d from the axis produces a moment; the angular impulse changes the angular momentum about that axis.

Eg. Bar m = 2 kg, L = 1 m, pivoted at one end: I = mL²/3 = 0.667 kg·m². A 100 N force at the free end for 0.01 s gives angular impulse 1 N·m·s, so ω = 1.5 rad/s.

For a system with several rotating parts, add the angular impulses about the same axis; internal impulses cancel in pairs.
