1Polar coordinates

r = r r̂
v = (dr/dt) r̂ + r(dθ/dt) θ̂
a = [d²r/dt² − r(dθ/dt)²] r̂ + [r d²θ/dt² + 2(dr/dt)(dθ/dt)] θ̂
Eg. r = 2t m, θ = t² rad; at t = 1 s: vr = 2 m/s, vθ = 4 m/s; ar = −8 m/s², aθ = 12 m/s².

2Friction and inclined planes

Static: fs ≤ μsN; impending: fs = μsN
Kinetic: fk = μkN,   μs > μk
Friction angle: tan φ = μ; angle of repose: tan θr = μs
Eg. Block on θ = 30°, μ = 0.2: a = g(sinθ − μcosθ) = 3.21 m/s²; at rest, friction is mg sinθ up the plane.

3Constraints and degrees of freedom

A constraint restricts the possible motion. Holonomic constraints depend on coordinates (and possibly time); non-holonomic involve velocities or inequalities. Scleronomic = time-independent; rheonomic = time-dependent.

Pulley: x1 + x2 = L
Incline: y = x tan θ
Circle: x² + y² = R²
DOF = 3n − k  (n particles, k constraints)
Eg. Two particles (6 coordinates) connected by a rigid rod (1 constraint) → 5 DOF; a bead on a circle has 1 DOF (θ).

4Connected blocks on two inclines

Masses m1 and m2 on inclines α and β, connected over a pulley at the apex (frictionless):

a = (m1sinα − m2sinβ)g/(m1+m2)
T = m1(g sinα − a)
Eg. m1 = 5 kg, α = 30°, m2 = 3 kg, β = 20° → a = 1.81 m/s², T = 15.5 N.

5Relative motion: rain and walking

vrain/man = vrain − vman

For rain falling vertically at vr and a person walking horizontally at vm, the rain appears to come from an angle:

tan θ = vr/vm,   |vrel| = √(vr² + vm²)
Eg. vr = 10 m/s down, vm = 5 m/s forward → relative rain 11.2 m/s at 63.4° from the horizontal; tilt the umbrella forward.

6Cylindrical coordinates

r = ρ ρ̂ + z ẑ
v = (dρ/dt) ρ̂ + ρ(dφ/dt) φ̂ + (dz/dt) ẑ
a = [d²ρ/dt² − ρ(dφ/dt)²] ρ̂
  + [ρ d²φ/dt² + 2(dρ/dt)(dφ/dt)] φ̂ + (d²z/dt²) ẑ

Transform: x = ρ cos φ, y = ρ sin φ, z = z; ρ² = x² + y², tan φ = y/x.

Eg. ρ = 2 m, φ = 90°, z = 3 m → (x, y, z) = (0, 2, 3); a point on the y-axis has ρ = |y|.

7Inertial and non-inertial frames

An inertial frame moves with constant velocity; Newton's laws hold. A frame accelerating with a0 is non-inertial; add a pseudo force −ma0.

Galilean: r' = r − Vt, v' = v − V, a' = a (V constant)
Rotating frame: Fpseudo = mω²r (outward) − 2mω×v (Coriolis)
Eg. Lift accelerating up at a: apparent weight N = m(g+a); free fall: N = 0; accelerating down: N = m(g−a).

82D motion: projectile and circular motion

x = u cosθ t,   y = u sinθ t − ½gt²
T = 2u sinθ/g,   H = u²sin²θ/(2g),   R = u²sin2θ/g

Circular motion: v = ωr, at = αr, an = v²/r = ω²r. For uniform circular motion, speed is constant but velocity changes direction.

Eg. u = 20 m/s, θ = 30° → R = 35.3 m, H = 5.1 m, T = 2.04 s; a car on r = 50 m at 15 m/s has an = 4.5 m/s².

93D motion and relative motion

rA = rB + rA/B
vA = vB + vA/B
aA = aB + aA/B

For a projectile in 3D, use a = (0, 0, −g) and integrate each component. For a frame moving with constant V, aA/B = 0 and the relative velocity is constant.

Eg. A boat moves 5 m/s across a river with current 3 m/s: ground speed √(5²+3²) = 5.83 m/s at 31° to the bank; crossing 100 m takes 20 s.

10Circular motion dynamics

Fc = mv²/r = mω²r
Conical pendulum: T cosθ = mg, T sinθ = mv²/r
v = √(gL sinθ tanθ),   Tperiod = 2π√(L cosθ/g)

Banked curve with no friction: tan θ = v²/(rg). The normal force provides the horizontal centripetal component; the vertical component balances weight.

Eg. Car on r = 100 m at v = 20 m/s: tanθ = 400/(100×9.81) = 0.408 → θ = 22.2°; a conical pendulum L = 1 m, θ = 30° has v = 1.68 m/s.

11Banked curve with friction

vmax² = rg(tanθ + μ)/(1 − μ tanθ)
vmin² = rg(tanθ − μ)/(1 + μ tanθ)

Friction acts down the slope at vmax (tendency to slide out) and up the slope at vmin (tendency to slide in). If the numerator of vmin² is negative, no minimum speed is needed.

Eg. r = 100 m, θ = 20°, μ = 0.2: vmax = √[100(9.81)(0.364+0.2)/(1−0.0728)] = 24.4 m/s.

12Spherical coordinates

r = r r̂
v = (dr/dt) r̂ + r(dθ/dt) θ̂ + r sin θ (dφ/dt) φ̂
ar = d²r/dt² − r(dθ/dt)² − r sin²θ (dφ/dt)²
aθ = r d²θ/dt² + 2(dr/dt)(dθ/dt) − r sinθ cosθ (dφ/dt)²
aφ = r sinθ d²φ/dt² + 2 sinθ (dr/dt)(dφ/dt) + 2r cosθ (dθ/dt)(dφ/dt)
Eg. r = 2, θ = 60°, φ = 30° → x = 1.5, y = 0.866, z = 1; ρ = 1.732.

13Newton's laws of motion

LawStatement
IA body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless a net external force acts; this defines an inertial frame.
IIF = dp/dt = ma for constant mass; in components Fx=max, etc.
IIIAction and reaction are equal, opposite and along the same line; they act on different bodies.
Fnet = ΣF = ma,   Fx = m d²x/dt²
Eg. m = 2 kg, F = (4, 6) N → a = (2, 3) m/s²; the acceleration is along the net force.

14Newton's laws: connected bodies

For two masses m1 > m2 over a light frictionless pulley:

m1g − T = m1a
T − m2g = m2a
a = (m1−m2)g/(m1+m2)
T = 2m1m2g/(m1+m2)
Eg. 5 kg and 3 kg → a = 2.45 m/s², T = 36.8 N; for a block on an incline, resolve along and perpendicular to the plane.

15Worked coordinate conversion

Convert (x, y, z) = (1, 1, √2) to cylindrical and spherical coordinates, and write the position vector.

ρ = √(1²+1²) = √2,   φ = tan−1(1/1) = 45°
r = √(1²+1²+2) = 2
θ = cos−1(z/r) = cos−1(√2/2) = 45°
Eg. (ρ, φ, z) = (√2, 45°, √2); (r, θ, φ) = (2, 45°, 45°); r = i + j + √2 k.

16Constrained motion examples

ConstraintEquationDOF
Movable pulleysA + 2sB = L1
Block on wedgey = x tan θ1
Bead on a hoopx² + y² = R²1
Two particles, rigid rod(x1−x2)² + (y1−y2)² = L²5

Differentiate a constraint to relate velocities/accelerations: for the pulley, vA + 2vB = 0 and aA + 2aB = 0.

Eg. If A moves down at 2 m/s, B moves up at 1 m/s; if A accelerates at 3 m/s², B accelerates at 1.5 m/s².

17Vertical circular motion

Top: T + mg = mv²/r
Bottom: T − mg = mv²/r
Minimum at top: vtop = √(gr), T = 0
Energy: ½mvb² = ½mvt² + mg(2r)
vb = √(5gr)
Eg. r = 1.5 m → vtop = 3.84 m/s, vbottom = 8.58 m/s; the string must not go slack at the top.

18Coordinate systems and transformations

SystemCoordinatesCartesian form
Cartesian(x, y, z)x, y, z
Polar (2D)(r, θ)x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ
Cylindrical(ρ, φ, z)x = ρ cos φ, y = ρ sin φ, z = z
Spherical(r, θ, φ)x = r sin θ cos φ, y = r sin θ sin φ, z = r cos θ

Ranges: polar/cylindrical ρ ≥ 0, 0 ≤ φ < 2π; spherical r ≥ 0, 0 ≤ θ ≤ π, 0 ≤ φ < 2π; ρ = r sin θ.

Eg. (x, y, z) = (1, 1, √2) → ρ = √2, φ = 45°, z = √2; r = 2, θ = 45°, φ = 45°.

19Cartesian kinematics in 2D and 3D

r = x i + y j + z k
v = (dx/dt) i + (dy/dt) j + (dz/dt) k
a = (d²x/dt²) i + (d²y/dt²) j + (d²z/dt²) k

Each Cartesian component is independent; x motion does not affect y or z. In 2D, drop the z terms.

Eg. r = (2t, t², 3) m → v = (2, 2t, 0) m/s, a = (0, 2, 0) m/s²; at t = 2 s, v = (2, 4, 0).

20Unit-vector derivatives

The unit vectors change direction as the coordinates change; this produces the centripetal and Coriolis terms.

dr̂/dt = (dθ/dt) θ̂ + sinθ (dφ/dt) φ̂
dθ̂/dt = −(dθ/dt) r̂ + cosθ (dφ/dt) φ̂
dφ̂/dt = −(dφ/dt)(sinθ r̂ + cosθ θ̂)

Substituting these into v = dr/dt and a = dv/dt gives the polar, cylindrical and spherical acceleration formulas.

22Rotating frames and Coriolis

In a frame rotating with angular velocity ω, the extra inertial forces are:

Centrifugal: mω²r (outward)
Coriolis: −2mω × v

The Coriolis acceleration is perpendicular to both ω and v; its horizontal component on Earth is ac = 2ωv sin λ, where λ is the latitude.

Eg. ω = 7.29×10−5 rad/s, v = 100 m/s, λ = 30° → ac = 2(7.29×10−5)(100)(0.5) = 7.29×10−3 m/s².

23Projectile from a height

x = u cosθ t
y = h + u sinθ t − ½gt²

Set y = 0 to find the time of flight; the horizontal range is R = u cosθ · t. At impact, vx = u cosθ, vy = u sinθ − gt.

Eg. h = 10 m, u = 20 m/s, θ = 30°: 0 = 10 + 10t − 4.905t² → t = 2.77 s, R = 48.0 m; impact speed ≈ 21.6 m/s.
