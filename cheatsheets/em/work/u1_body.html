1Varignon's theorem

Varignon: the moment of a resultant about a point equals the sum of the moments of its components about the same point.

MO = Σ Mi = Σ (ri × Fi)
2D:   MO = x Fy − y Fx

Use components when the perpendicular distance is awkward. The coordinates (x, y) are measured from the moment point O, and each term carries its own sign.

Eg. F = 50 N at 30° applied at (2, 1) m: MO = 2(25) − 1(43.3) = +6.7 N·m (CCW).

Proof: write F = F1 + F2. Then MO = r × F = r × F1 + r × F2 = M1 + M2; extend by induction to any number of components.

Eg. F = (30, 40) N at (2, 1) m: MO = 2(40) − 1(30) = 50 N·m CCW.

2Force-system reduction

Any force system can be replaced at a chosen point O by a resultant force and a couple:

R = ΣF,   MO = ΣMO = Σ(r × F)

Moving a force from A to O adds the moment r × F; do not forget it.

CaseEquivalent system
R ≠ 0, M = 0single force through O
R ≠ 0, M ≠ 0force + couple; if R ⊥ M, a single force at d = M/R
R = 0, M ≠ 0pure couple
R ≠ 0, M ∥ Rwrench (3D)
Eg. At O, R = 50 N to the right and MO = 20 N·m CCW. Since R ⊥ M, replace by a single 50 N force at d = 20/50 = 0.4 m above O.

Equivalent systems: same R and same MO; verify by taking moments about a second point.

3Free-body diagrams

An FBD isolates one body and shows every external force on it: applied loads, weight at the centroid, support reactions and friction. Never show internal forces or forces the body exerts on something else.

isolate→axes→all external forces→label unknowns

Procedure: (1) choose the body; (2) draw its outline; (3) add applied loads and weight at the centroid; (4) replace supports by reactions; (5) add friction opposite impending motion; (6) label unknowns and axes.

Eg. A beam on a pin at A and a roller at B has reactions Ax, Ay, By; the roller reaction is normal to the surface.

43D equilibrium

ΣFx = ΣFy = ΣFz = 0
ΣMx = ΣMy = ΣMz = 0

Vector form: ΣF = 0 and ΣMO = 0. Six independent equations allow up to six unknown reactions. Draw a 3D FBD and use unit vectors or direction cosines.

Common 3D supports: roller 1, ball-and-socket 3, 3D hinge 5, fixed 6.

Vector equations: ΣF = 0 gives three scalar equations; ΣMO = 0 gives three. A 3D couple is a free vector with components Mx, My, Mz.

Eg. F = (2, −3, 4) N at r = (1, 2, 0) m: MO = r × F = (8, −4, −7) N·m.

52D equilibrium

ΣFx = 0,   ΣFy = 0,   ΣMA = 0

These are three independent equations. Alternatives: ΣMA = ΣMB = 0 plus ΣFx = 0 if AB is not perpendicular to x; or ΣMA = ΣMB = ΣMC = 0 if A, B, C are not collinear.

Determinacy: 3 unknowns → statically determinate; more than 3 → statically indeterminate; fewer than 3 → unstable or a mechanism.

Eg. Simply supported beam with load P at midspan: Ax = 0, Ay = By = P/2.
Eg. Beam AB = 4 m, pin at A, roller at B, 10 N load 1 m from A. ΣMB = 0: −Ay(4) + 10(3) = 0 → Ay = 7.5 N; By = 2.5 N.

Independent equations: three in 2D; any fourth is a linear combination and adds no information.

7Lami's theorem

For a particle in equilibrium under three concurrent coplanar forces, each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two:

F1 / sin α = F2 / sin β = F3 / sin γ

α is the angle between F2 and F3 (opposite F1), and similarly for β and γ. The three lines of action must meet at the particle.

Eg. Three forces at 120° with F1 = 100 N → F2 = F3 = 100 N, because all three sines are equal.

Derivation: the three forces form a closed triangle. By the sine rule, each force is proportional to the sine of the opposite angle: F1/sin α = F2/sin β = F3/sin γ.

Eg. A 100 N weight is held by two ropes at 30° and 60° to the horizontal; the force triangle gives both tensions directly.

8Location of the resultant

After finding R = ΣF and MO = ΣMO, the resultant line of action is at perpendicular distance d = MO/R from O, when R ≠ 0.

For parallel forces, the resultant acts at the centre of parallel forces:

x̄ = Σ xiFi / ΣFi,   ȳ = Σ yiFi / ΣFi
Eg. Downward forces 10 N at x = 0 and 20 N at x = 3 m → R = 30 N at x = 2 m.

Derivation: for a single resultant, MO = R d (R and M perpendicular), so d = MO/R. For parallel forces, taking moments about the origin gives x̄ ΣF = Σ x F.

Eg. R = 30 N at x = 2 m gives MO = 60 N·m; check d = M/R = 2 m.

9Force systems: the vocabulary

Force is a vector: magnitude in N, direction, and point of application. A particle has negligible size; a rigid body does not deform.

Systems: collinear (same line), concurrent (meet at one point), coplanar (same plane), parallel, non-coplanar. Forces are external (applied loads and reactions) or internal (cancel within the body).

The resultant replaces the system; the equilibrant is equal and opposite to the resultant. Equilibrium requires R = 0 and M = 0.

Newton's lawContent
IA body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless a resultant force acts.
IIF = ma; in statics a = 0, so ΣF = 0.
IIIAction and reaction are equal, opposite and collinear.

Units: 1 kgf = 9.81 N; 1 kN = 103 N; moment in N·m. A force's line of action is part of its specification.

Eg. A 10 N force at 30° and a 10 N force at 120° have the same magnitude but different components and lines of action; both must be specified.

11Distributed loads

The resultant of a distributed load is the area under the load diagram and acts at its centroid.

W = ∫ w(x) dx,   x̄ = ∫ x w(x) dx / W

UDL w over length L: W = wL at L/2. Triangular load from 0 to w: W = wL/2 at 2L/3 from the zero end (L/3 from the heavy end).

Derivation: dW = w(x) dx; W = ∫0L w dx; x̄ = (1/W)∫0L x w dx. For w(x) = w x/L: W = wL/2 and x̄ = 2L/3 from the zero end.

Eg. A triangular load rising from 0 to 6 kN/m over 3 m: W = 9 kN at 2 m from the zero end.

13Components and resultant

2D components: resolve along perpendicular axes.

Fx = F cos θ,   Fy = F sin θ
F = √(Fx2 + Fy2),   θ = tan−1(Fy/Fx)

3D: use direction angles α, β, γ from the positive axes. The direction cosines satisfy cos2α + cos2β + cos2γ = 1.

Fx = F cos α,   Fy = F cos β,   Fz = F cos γ

Concurrent resultant: Rx = ΣFx, Ry = ΣFy (and Rz); then find magnitude and direction.

Eg. F = 100 N at 30° → Fx = 86.6 N, Fy = 50 N.

Two-force resultant: R = √(P2 + Q2 + 2PQ cos α), where α is the angle between P and Q; direction tan θ = Q sin α / (P + Q cos α).

Eg. 80 N at 210° → Fx = −69.3 N, Fy = −40 N (third quadrant).

14Moment of a force

Moment about O: MO = F d, where d is the perpendicular distance from O to the line of action. Unit: N·m. Counter-clockwise is positive.

Vector form: MO = r × F; the direction follows the right-hand rule. If the line of action passes through O, d = 0 and MO = 0.

Derivation (2D): with r = (x, y) and F = (Fx, Fy), r × F = (x Fy − y Fx) k. The sign gives the sense about the z-axis.

Eg. A vertical 30 N force at x = 2 m gives M = 60 N·m; at x = 0, M = 0.

15Couples

A couple is two equal, opposite, parallel forces separated by a perpendicular distance d. Its resultant force is zero, but its moment is M = F d.

A couple is a free vector: it has the same moment about every point and can be moved anywhere on the rigid body. Equivalent couples have the same M.

Eg. F = 20 N, d = 0.5 m → M = 10 N·m.

Derivation: MO = r1 × F + r2 × (−F) = (r1 − r2) × F = d × F. Only the separation d matters, so the moment is independent of O.

Eg. Two 15 N forces 0.4 m apart give M = 6 N·m; moving the couple elsewhere does not change M.

16Moment about an axis (3D)

The moment of a force about an axis through O with unit direction u is the projection of MO = r × F onto that axis:

Maxis = u · (r × F)

r runs from any point on the axis to the point of application; u is a unit vector along the axis. The result is a signed scalar.

Eg. u = k, r = (2, 1, 0), F = (3, 4, 0) → Mz = 2(4) − 1(3) = 5 N·m.

Derivation: MO = r × F; its component along u is MO · u = u · (r × F), the scalar triple product.

Eg. MO = (8, −4, −7) N·m, u = (0, 0, 1) → Mz = −7 N·m.
