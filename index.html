2. ACADEMIC & EXAMINATION ARCHITECTURE
Evaluation & Scoring Blueprint
  • Theory Papers (40 Int + 60 Ext): 40 marks internal continuous evaluation (mid-terms, assignments, attendance) + 60 marks 3-hour university end-term exam. Minimum 40% aggregate required to pass.
  • NUES Coursework (EVSL / PD): 100 marks continuous evaluation managed internally by subject faculty. Zero external university written exam.
  • Question Paper Pattern (60 Marks): 9 questions total. Q1 (12M compulsory) covers entire syllabus. Units I–IV each feature 2 questions (12M each); students attempt exactly one per unit.
Degree & Credit Standards
  • Graduation Target: Minimum 160 qualifying credits across 8 semesters (176–178 max).
  • Minor Specialization: 20 optional credits from Sem 3 to 7 via electives / MOOCs (does NOT affect CGPA).

3. DETAILED SYLLABUS BREAKDOWN (PART 1: CORE TECH)

Programming for Problem Solving & Lab

PPS / PPS LAB
Code: ICT 101 T & ICT 151 / 101 P  |  Credits: 3 + 1 = 4C  |  L-P: 3-2
Faculty: Ms. Alka (Theory) & Mr. Ankit Jain (Lab in Room 2310)
Weekly Slots: Wed 12:25, Thu 11:30, Fri 8:15  |  Lab: Tue 11:30–1:20
Course Outcomes: CO1: Structured C & standards. CO2: Functions, arrays, search/sort. CO3: Pointers, dynamic memory, file I/O. CO4: ADTs (linked lists, stacks, queues, binary trees).
Unit I (Foundations & Standards): Structure of C program, basic types (int, char, float, double, bool, fixed-width ints), const, #define, operators & precedence, type conversions (implicit/explicit), I/O (printf, scanf, format specifiers), compound blocks, decision making (if-else, switch), loops (while, do-while, for), jumping (break, continue, goto), flowcharts, dry runs, and C89, C99, C11, C23 extensions.
Unit II (Functions, Arrays & Strings): Functions, parameter passing (by value, recursion), inline/macro functions, storage classes (auto, static, extern, register, thread_local), scope & lifetime, multi-dim arrays, linear & binary search, bubble/selection/insertion sort, string I/O (fgets, fputs), string manipulation (strlen, strcpy, strcat, strcmp), and command-line arguments.
Unit III (Pointers, Dynamic Memory & Files): Structs, nested structs, array of structs, unions, enums, bit-fields, memory layout/alignment, pointer arithmetic, pointer to pointer, dynamic memory (malloc, calloc, realloc, free), memory leak prevention, file I/O (fopen, fclose, fprintf, fscanf, fread, fwrite), and errno handling.
Unit IV (Data Structures & ADTs): Singly and doubly linked lists, stacks (infix-postfix, parenthesis balancing), queues (circular, priority), binary tree traversals (pre, in, postorder), function pointers, generic code via macros, assertions (_Static_assert), and header hygiene.
Textbooks: B.W. Kernighan & D.M. Ritchie (2nd Ed.); Jens Gustedt, Modern C (2025). Lab: Minimum 16 practicals.

System Modeling Techniques - I

SMT-1
Code: ICT 107  |  Credits: 3C  |  L-P: 3-0
Faculty: Dr. Suryya Farhat  |  Weekly Slots: Mon 9:10, Tue 1:20, Thu 10:05
Course Outcomes: CO1: Multivariable calculus & 1st order ODEs. CO2: Higher ODEs & special functions. CO3: Matrices, eigenvalues & factorizations. CO4: Vector calculus.
Unit I (Multivariable Calculus & 1st Order ODEs): Functions of several variables, partial derivatives, chain rule, Jacobians, Taylor/Maclaurin series, maxima/minima, Lagrange multipliers. 1st order ODEs: separable, exact, integrating factors, Bernoulli equations, orthogonal trajectories.
Unit II (Higher ODEs & Special Functions): Higher-order linear ODEs with constant coeffs, variation of parameters, Cauchy-Euler equations, Wronskian. Power series solutions: Bessel and Legendre polynomials, Gamma and Beta functions.
Unit III (Linear Algebra & Factorizations): Systems of linear equations, Gauss-Jordan, rank, vector spaces, eigenvalues/eigenvectors, Cayley-Hamilton theorem, quadratic forms, Gram-Schmidt, and LU/Cholesky decomposition.
Unit IV (Vector Calculus & Field Theorems): Vector differential calculus (grad, div, curl), line/surface/volume integrals, Green's theorem, Gauss divergence theorem, and Stokes' theorem.
Textbook: Erwin Kreyszig, Advanced Engineering Mathematics (10th Ed., Wiley, 2011).

Basics of Electrical Engineering & Lab

BEE & BEE LAB
Code: ICT 103 T & ICT 103 P  |  Credits: 3 + 1 = 4C  |  L-P: 3-2
Faculty: Ms. Pooja  |  Weekly Slots: Mon 1:20, Thu 1:20, Fri 1:20  |  Lab: Wed / Fri 11:30–1:20
Course Outcomes: CO1: Kirchhoff's laws & DC circuits. CO2: Network theorems & transients. CO3: AC phasors & 3-phase circuits. CO4: Electrical machines & safety.
Unit I: Charge, current, voltage, energy, power, sources, Ohm’s law, series/parallel resistors, KCL, KVL, circuit modeling, and power calculations.
Unit II: Nodal & mesh analysis, superposition, Thevenin’s, Norton’s, and Maximum Power Transfer theorems, transient analysis of RL and RC circuits.
Unit III: Phasors, complex impedance/admittance, AC power (real, reactive, complex, power factor correction), RLC resonance, and balanced 3-phase circuits.
Unit IV: Magnetic circuits, Faraday's/Lenz's laws, transformers, DC motors/generators, 3-phase induction motors (torque-slip), safety, and data center energy auditing.
Textbooks: Alexander & Sadiku (7th Ed., 2022); D.P. Kothari & I.J. Nagrath. Lab: Min 10 experiments.

3. DETAILED SYLLABUS BREAKDOWN (PART 2: APPLIED SCIENCES)

Engineering Physics - I & Lab

PHL & PHP
Code: ICT 111 T & ICT 111 P  |  Credits: 3 + 1 = 4C  |  L-P: 3-2
Faculty: Dr. Mamta Gupta  |  Weekly Slots: Mon 10:05, Wed 11:30, Thu 8:15  |  Lab: Mon/Fri 11:30–1:20
Course Outcomes: CO1: Mechanics & reference frames. CO2: Conservation laws & central forces. CO3: Damped/forced oscillations. CO4: Rigid body dynamics.
Unit I: Coordinate systems (Cartesian, polar, cylindrical, spherical), Newton's laws, friction, constraints, and 2D/3D motion.
Unit II: Work done, potential energy, momentum conservation, torque, angular momentum, central forces, Kepler’s planetary laws, and satellite orbits.
Unit III: SHM, damped oscillations, forced oscillations, resonance, quality factor (Q), rotating frames, and Coriolis force.
Unit IV: Center of mass, moment of inertia, parallel/perpendicular axes theorems, rotational energy, rolling motion, and gyroscopic effects.
Textbooks: Kleppner & Kolenkow, An Introduction to Mechanics; Serway & Jewett. Lab: Min 10 exercises.

Engineering Mechanics

EM
Code: ICT 119  |  Credits: 3C  |  L-P: 3-0
Faculty: Mr. Naman Bajpai  |  Weekly Slots: Mon 8:15, Tue 10:05, Thu 2:15
Course Outcomes: CO1: Force equilibrium. CO2: Plane trusses & friction. CO3: Particle kinematics/kinetics. CO4: Rigid body dynamics.
Unit I: Force systems, Varignon's theorem, couples, free-body diagrams, and 2D/3D static equilibrium equations.
Unit II: Plane trusses (method of joints, sections), friction laws, angle of repose, cone of friction, belt and collar friction.
Unit III: Rectilinear/curvilinear motion, D'Alembert’s principle, work-energy, impulse-momentum, impact, and coefficient of restitution.
Unit IV: Rigid body motion, relative velocity/acceleration, instantaneous center of zero velocity, and fixed-axis rotation.
Textbook: A.K. Tayal, Engineering Mechanics; Timoshenko & Young.

Communication Skills

CSL
Code: ICT 113  |  Credits: 3C  |  L-P: 3-0
Faculty: Dr. Samikshya  |  Weekly Slots: Tue 9:10, Wed 8:15, Thu 12:25
Course Outcomes: CO1: English grammar & vocabulary. CO2: Paragraph, précis & writing clarity. CO3: Formal correspondence. CO4: Critical speech comprehension.
Unit I: Parts of speech, sentence transformation, subject-verb agreement, tenses, voice, speech, vocabulary, technical terms, and common errors.
Unit II: Effective writing, topic sentences, coherence, paraphrasing, summarizing, précis writing, and editing.
Unit III: Meeting documentation (agenda, minutes), business letters, applications, notices, memoranda, press releases, and professional e-mails.
Unit IV: Reading strategies (skimming, scanning). Critical speech analysis: (1) Steven Spielberg's Harvard Commencement Address (2016); (2) Johan Rockström's TED Talk on Planetary Boundaries.
Textbooks: Wren & Martin; Meenakshi Raman, Technical Communication (4th Ed., Oxford, 2022).

Environmental Studies & Lab

EVSL & EVS LAB
Code: EM 117 T & ICT 117 P  |  Credits: 2 + 1 = 3C (NUES)
Faculty: Dr. Kavita Gupta  |  Weekly Slots: Tue 2:15, Wed 1:20, Thu 9:10  |  Lab: Mon/Wed
Course Outcomes: CO1: Ecological systems. CO2: Natural resources. CO3: Pollution & E-waste. CO4: Environmental law & field studies.
Unit I: Ecosystem structure, energy flow, biogeochemical cycles, biodiversity conservation, hot-spots, and ecosystem services.
Unit II: Renewable & non-renewable resources, water management, land degradation, mining impacts, and sustainable energy.
Unit III: Air, water, soil, noise pollution, Electronic Waste (E-Waste) management in IT, toxicology, climate change, and smog.
Unit IV: Environmental legislation, EIA principles, UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), and mandatory field visit report.
Textbook: Erach Bharucha, Environmental Studies (3rd Ed., 2020). 10 practicals & field report.

Engineering Graphics - I Lab

EGL
Code: ICT 123  |  Credits: 1C  |  L-P: 0-2
Faculty: Mr. Naman Bajpai  |  Weekly Slot: Fri 9:10–11:00 (2-Hour Block)
Course Outcomes: CO1: BIS codes & projection of points. CO2: Straight line projections. CO3: Plane projections. CO4: Solid projections & surface development.
Unit I: BIS drawing standards, scales, dimensioning, orthographic projections, and projection of points in all quadrants.
Unit II: Projection of straight lines (traces, rotation method, auxiliary planes, true inclinations).
Unit III: Projection of regular planes (parallel, inclined, oblique planes, auxiliary planes).
Unit IV: Projection of solids (prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones), and lateral surface development (parallel, radial line methods).
Textbook: N.D. Bhatt, Engineering Drawing (53rd Ed., Charotar Publishing).
