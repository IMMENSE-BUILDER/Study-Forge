2. ACADEMIC & EXAMINATION ARCHITECTURE
Evaluation & Scoring Blueprint
  • Theory Papers (40 Int + 60 Ext): 40 marks internal continuous evaluation (mid-terms, assignments, attendance) + 60 marks 3-hour university end-term exam. Minimum 40% aggregate required to pass.
  • NUES Coursework (EVSL / PD): 100 marks continuous evaluation managed internally by subject faculty. Zero external university written exam.
  • Question Paper Pattern (60 Marks): 9 questions total. Q1 (12M compulsory) covers entire syllabus. Units I–IV each feature 2 questions (12M each); students attempt exactly one per unit.
Degree & Credit Standards
  • Graduation Target: Minimum 160 qualifying credits across 8 semesters (176–178 max).
  • Minor Specialization: 20 optional credits from Sem 3 to 7 via electives / MOOCs (does NOT affect CGPA).

3. DETAILED SYLLABUS BREAKDOWN (PART 1: CORE TECH)

Programming for Problem Solving & Lab

PPS / PPS LAB
Code: ICT 101 & ICT 151 / 101 P  |  Credits: 3 + 1 = 4C  |  L-P: 3-2
Marking: 40 Continuous Eval + 60 Term-End Exam  |  Faculty: Ms. Alka (Theory) & Mr. Ankit Jain (Lab)
Weekly Slots: Wed 12:25, Thu 11:30, Fri 8:15  |  Lab: Tue 11:30–1:20
Course Outcomes: CO1: Simple algorithms & C implementation. CO2: Branching, iteration, recursion & functions. CO3: Arrays, pointers, structures & unions. CO4: Divide-and-conquer function decomposition.
Unit I (C Foundations, Standards & Control Flow): Structure of C program, basic data types (int, char, float, double, bool, fixed-width integers), declarations & initializations, const, #define, operators & expressions (arithmetic, relational, logical, bitwise, assignment, conditional, inc/dec), type conversions (implicit/explicit), I/O (printf, scanf, format specifiers), compound statements, if-else, switch, loops (while, do-while, for, infinite loops, early exit), break, continue, goto, return, algorithm development with flowcharts & pseudocode, tracing & dry runs, structured programming, and C89, C99, C11, C23 extensions and usage.
Unit II (Functions, Modular Design, Arrays & Searching): Function declaration, definition & invocation, argument passing (by value, recursion), inline & macro functions, storage classes (auto, static, extern, register, thread_local), scope & lifetime of variables, header files & modular program design, 1D and 2D arrays (memory layout), bubble/selection/insertion sort, linear & binary search, string I/O (gets, puts, fgets, fputs), string manipulation (strlen, strcpy, strcat, strcmp), arrays of strings, command-line arguments, and root finding of a positive floating point number.
Unit III (Structures, Unions, Pointers & File I/O): Structures (definition, nested, array, pointer to struct), unions (use-cases, memory layout), enumerated types (enum, typedef enum), bit fields, memory layout & alignment, pointers (declaration, dereferencing, pointer arithmetic, arrays vs pointers, pointer-to-pointer), dynamic memory allocation (malloc, calloc, realloc, free, leak prevention), NULL & null pointer checks, compound literals, polynomial representation & arithmetic with floating point coeffs, matrix arithmetic, File I/O (text/binary, fopen, fclose, fprintf, fscanf, fread, fwrite, fgets, fputs), error handling via errno, and command-line parameters.
Unit IV (Data Structures, ADTs & Advanced C): Abstract Data Types (ADTs), singly and doubly linked lists, stack (array & linked list implementation, infix-to-postfix conversion, parenthesis balancing), queue (linear, circular, priority), list traversal/insertion/deletion, binary tree basics (creation, inorder, preorder, postorder traversals), function pointers, pointers to arrays & functions, generic code via macros, _Generic for type-generic programming, assertions & diagnostics (assert, _Static_assert), safer macros & expressions, forward declarations, program segmentation, header hygiene, and recursion.
Textbooks: B.W. Kernighan & D.M. Ritchie — The C Programming Language (2nd Ed.); Jens Gustedt — Modern C. Lab: At least 10 practical exercises.

System Modeling Techniques - I

SMT-1
Code: ICT 107  |  Credits: 3C  |  L-P: 3-0
Marking: 40 Continuous Eval + 60 Term-End Exam (Q1 compulsory 12m + 4 units × 12m)
Faculty: Dr. Suryya Farhat  |  Weekly Slots: Mon 9:10, Tue 1:20, Thu 10:05
Course Outcomes: CO1: Multivariable differential calculus & optimization. CO2: ODE analytical & power series solutions. CO3: Linear equations, eigenvalues & matrix factorizations. CO4: Vector differential & integral theorems.
Unit I (Multivariable Calculus & 1st Order ODEs): Functions of several variables, partial derivatives, chain rule, implicit differentiation, exact differentials, Jacobians & coordinate transformations, Taylor's & Maclaurin's series, maxima, minima & saddle points, Lagrange multipliers, Leibniz rule (differentiation under integral sign); Ordinary differential equations: geometrical interpretation, direction fields, Euler's method, separable, exact, integrating factors, linear differential equations, Bernoulli equation, orthogonal trajectories, and engineering applications.
Unit II (Higher ODEs & Power Series Solutions): Higher-order linear differential equations with constant coefficients, differential operator method, Euler-Cauchy equations, Wronskian, method of variation of parameters, free oscillations of mass-spring systems; Power series solutions: Bessel's equation & Bessel functions of the first kind, Legendre's equation & Legendre polynomials, recurrence relations & properties, Gamma and Beta functions and their properties.
Unit III (Linear Algebra, Matrices & Factorizations): Matrices & determinants, Gauss elimination, Gauss-Jordan elimination, rank of a matrix, linear independence, vector spaces, systems of linear equations (existence & uniqueness, Cramer's rule), eigenvalues & eigenvectors, symmetric, skew-symmetric & orthogonal matrices, eigenbases, matrix diagonalization, quadratic forms, Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, Cayley-Hamilton theorem (without proof), LU decomposition & Cholesky decomposition with engineering applications.
Unit IV (Vector Calculus & Field Theorems): Vector and scalar point functions, space curves, arc length, curvature, gradient, directional derivative, divergence, curl, line integrals, path independence, double and triple integrals, surface integrals, Green's theorem, Gauss divergence theorem, Stokes' theorem, and engineering applications.
Textbook: Erwin Kreyszig — Advanced Engineering Mathematics (10th Ed., John Wiley & Sons, 2011). Refs: K.F. Riley, K.A. Stroud, D.G. Zill, D.G. Duffy.

Basics of Electrical Engineering & Lab

BEE & BEE LAB
Code: ICT 103T / ICT 106T & ICT 103P / ICT 106P  |  Credits: 3 + 1 = 4C  |  L-P: 3-2
Marking: 40 Continuous Eval + 60 Term-End Exam (both Theory & Lab)
Faculty: Ms. Pooja  |  Weekly Slots: Mon 1:20, Thu 1:20, Fri 1:20  |  Lab: Wed / Fri 11:30–1:20
Course Outcomes: CO1: Kirchhoff's laws for resistive circuits. CO2: Inductive/capacitive circuits, transients & sinusoidal steady state. CO3: First-order filters & magnetic circuits. CO4: Design & operation of electrical machines.
Unit I (DC Circuit Fundamentals & Modeling): Electric charge, current, voltage, energy, power, independent & dependent sources, Ohm's law, series & parallel resistors, voltage & current division, ideal & real circuit elements, source transformations, Kirchhoff's current law, Kirchhoff's voltage law, basic circuit modeling, practical resistor behavior, and power calculations in resistive circuits.
Unit II (Network Theorems & Transient Analysis): Nodal analysis, mesh analysis, linearity & superposition theorem, Thevenin's theorem, Norton's theorem, maximum power transfer theorem, circuit simplification strategies, controlled sources in analysis, transient analysis of first-order circuits, natural & step response of RL circuits, and natural & step response of RC circuits.
Unit III (AC Circuits, Resonance & Three-Phase): Sinusoidal signals & phasors, impedance & admittance in frequency domain, node & mesh analysis in phasor domain, power in AC circuits (average, reactive, complex power, power factor & correction), resonance in series & parallel RLC circuits, balanced three-phase circuits, phase sequence, line & phase quantities, and power in three-phase loads.
Unit IV (Magnetic Circuits, Machines & Digital Infrastructure): Magnetic circuits, Faraday's & Lenz's laws, inductance & mutual inductance, ideal transformer model, real transformer behavior, energy conversion principles, operating principles of DC machines, types & characteristics of DC motors and generators, basic concepts of three-phase induction motors (torque and slip), circuit protection devices, electrical safety & grounding, power systems overview for digital infrastructure, and energy auditing & efficiency principles.
Textbooks: Charles K. Alexander & Matthew N.O. Sadiku — Fundamentals of Electric Circuits (7th Ed., McGraw-Hill, 2022); D.P. Kothari & I.J. Nagrath. Lab: At least 10 practical exercises.

3. DETAILED SYLLABUS BREAKDOWN (PART 2: APPLIED SCIENCES)

Engineering Physics - I & Lab

PHL & PHP
Code: ICT 111 T & ICT 111 P  |  Credits: 3 + 1 = 4C  |  L-P: 3-2
Faculty: Dr. Mamta Gupta  |  Weekly Slots: Mon 10:05, Wed 11:30, Thu 8:15  |  Lab: Mon/Fri 11:30–1:20
Course Outcomes: CO1: Mechanics & reference frames. CO2: Conservation laws & central forces. CO3: Damped/forced oscillations. CO4: Rigid body dynamics.
Unit I: Coordinate systems (Cartesian, polar, cylindrical, spherical), Newton's laws, friction, constraints, and 2D/3D motion.
Unit II: Work done, potential energy, momentum conservation, torque, angular momentum, central forces, Kepler’s planetary laws, and satellite orbits.
Unit III: SHM, damped oscillations, forced oscillations, resonance, quality factor (Q), rotating frames, and Coriolis force.
Unit IV: Center of mass, moment of inertia, parallel/perpendicular axes theorems, rotational energy, rolling motion, and gyroscopic effects.
Textbooks: Kleppner & Kolenkow, An Introduction to Mechanics; Serway & Jewett. Lab: Min 10 exercises.

Engineering Mechanics

EM
Code: ICT 119  |  Credits: 3C  |  L-P: 3-0
Marking: 40 Continuous Eval + 60 Term-End Exam (Q1 compulsory 12m + 4 units × 12m)
Faculty: Mr. Naman Bajpai  |  Weekly Slots: Mon 8:15, Tue 10:05, Thu 2:15
Course Outcomes: CO1: Force systems, equilibrium & distributed systems. CO2: Friction & plane engineering trusses. CO3: Kinematics & kinetics of particles. CO4: Kinematics & kinetics of rigid bodies.
Unit I (Force Systems & Static Equilibrium): Force System: introduction, force, principle of transmissibility of force, resultant of a force system, resolution of a force, moment of force about a line, Varignon's theorem, couple, resolution of force into force and a couple, properties of couple and engineering applications; Equilibrium: force body diagrams, equations of equilibrium and engineering applications, equilibrium of two force and three force members.
Unit II (Trusses & Engineering Friction): Structure: plane truss, perfect and imperfect truss, assumptions in truss analysis, analysis of perfect plane trusses by method of joints, method of section, and graphical method; Friction: static & kinetic friction, laws of dry friction, coefficient of friction, angle of friction, angle of repose, cone of friction, frictional lock, friction in flat pivot and collar bearing, and friction in flat belts.
Unit III (Kinematics & Kinetics of Particles): Kinematics of Particles: rectilinear motion, plane curvilinear motion, rectangular coordinates, normal and tangential coordinates; Kinetics of Particles: equations of motion, rectilinear & curvilinear motion, work-energy equation, conservation of energy, concept of impulse and momentum, conservation of momentum, impact of bodies, coefficient of restitution, and loss of energy during impact.
Unit IV (Kinematics & Kinetics of Rigid Bodies): Kinematics of Rigid Bodies: concept of rigid body, types of rigid body motion, absolute motion, introduction to relative velocity, relative acceleration (Corioli's component excluded), instantaneous center of zero velocity, velocity & acceleration; Kinetics of Rigid Bodies: equation of motion, translatory motion and fixed axis rotation, application of work-energy principles to rigid bodies, and conservation of energy.
Textbook: A.K. Tayal — Engineering Mechanics (Umesh Publications). Refs: K.L. Kumar, S. Timoshenko & D.H. Young, Irving H. Shames, Basudev Bhattacharya.

3. DETAILED SYLLABUS BREAKDOWN (PART 3: LABS & SKILLS)

Environmental Studies & Lab

EVSL & EVS LAB
Code: ICT 117T & ICT 117P  |  Credits: 2C (Theory) + Practical
Marking: 100 Marks Teachers Continuous Evaluation (NUES Qualifying, Non-Credit)
Faculty: Dr. Kavita Gupta  |  Weekly Slots: Tue 2:15, Wed 1:20, Thu 9:10  |  Lab: Mon/Wed
Course Outcomes: CO1: Environment as interconnected system. CO2: Renewable & non-renewable resources. CO3: Pollution types & climate change impacts. CO4: Regulations & environmental policies.
Unit I (Ecosystems & Biodiversity Conservation): Scope & importance of environmental studies, historical perspectives on human-environment interactions, basic ecological principles, levels of organization in ecology, ecosystem components (biotic & abiotic), energy flow in ecosystems, biogeochemical cycles, biodiversity and its conservation, importance & threats to biodiversity, and ecosystem services value.
Unit II (Natural Resources, Water & Land Degradation): Renewable and non-renewable resources, principles of sustainable resource use, water resources & management, global water distribution & availability, water pollution & management challenges, land resources, soil formation & types, soil degradation processes, land use patterns & environmental impacts, mineral resources extraction consequences, energy resources & environmental impacts, forests & wildlife resources, deforestation causes & effects, wildlife conservation principles, and role of protected areas.
Unit III (Pollution Control, E-Waste & Climate Change): Air, water, soil, noise, marine, and thermal pollution, hazardous waste management (sources, effects, mitigation for different pollution types), Electronic Waste (E-Waste) Management, environmental toxicology, public health and environment, climate change (greenhouse effect, greenhouse gases, global warming evidence & impacts), ozone layer depletion, acid rain, smog, and overview of major natural and man-made environmental disasters.
Unit IV (Legislation, EIA, Environmental Ethics & Case Studies): Overview of environmental legislation and policies, role of government agencies in environmental protection, concept & purpose of Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA), sustainable development, environmental ethics & philosophy (anthropocentrism, biocentrism, ecocentrism, deep ecology), population and environment, urbanization challenges, environmental movements & NGOs, public participation in decision-making, case studies of environmental challenges & solutions, and individual actions for sustainability.
Textbook: Erach Bharucha — Environmental Studies (3rd Ed., Orient Blackswan, 2020). Refs: Daniel B. Botkin, Rachel Carson (Silent Spring), Eugene P. Odum. NUES evaluation by concerned teacher.
